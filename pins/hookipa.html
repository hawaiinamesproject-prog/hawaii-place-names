Ho‘okipa Beach Park

Maui — Pāʻia

Place: Hoʻokipa, Maui

Type: Beach / surf break

Story it tells: A place whose name means “hospitality,” but whose conditions demand experience and respect.

Ho‘okipa Beach Park along Maui’s north shore, where steady winds and reef breaks define the coastline. Photo by dronepicr, via Flickr / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 2.0)

Hoʻokipa, meaning “hospitality” or “to welcome,” once described a wide sandy beach lined with trees along Maui’s north shore. In 1946, a tsunami reshaped the coastline, causing mass erosion and stripping much of the sand and trees along the shoreline.

Today, Hoʻokipa is defined less by sand and more by reef, rock, wind, and wave. A system of offshore reefs creates multiple surf breaks across the bay, while steady trade winds produce consistent, powerful conditions. Waves are typically largest in the winter, drawing experienced surfers, while the combination of wind and wave makes the area especially suited for high-level windsurfing.

Distinct breaks such as Pavilions, Middles, and The Point shape how the coastline is used, with The Point becoming one of the most well-known windsurfing launches. Under rare Kona wind conditions, the dynamics shift, creating different wave patterns and surf directions along the coast.

The name endures, though the landscape it once described has changed. Hoʻokipa remains a gathering place for ocean sports, spectators, and visitors traveling along the Hāna Highway. Hospitality now expressed less through a sheltered shoreline and more through a place that welcomes those willing to meet the ocean on its terms.
