Honolua Bay

Maui

Place: Honolua Bay, West Maui

Type: Bay / surf break / marine conservation area

Story it tells: A West Maui bay whose name reflects twin harbors, chiefly history, voyaging, surf culture, and marine life.

Honolua Bay on Maui’s northwest coast. Photo by Forest and Kim Starr, licensed under CC BY 3.0 via Wikimedia Commons.

Honolua Bay is a sheltered bay on Maui’s northwest coast, just north of Kapalua. The name Honolua is commonly translated as “two bays” or “twin harbors,” referring to the paired coastal inlets of Honolua and nearby Mokuleʻia. These bays are part of the broader ahupuaʻa of Honolua and belong to a historic West Maui coastline long used for travel, fishing, landing, and ocean access.

Honolua is also connected to Nā Honoapiʻilani, often translated as “the bays acquired by Piʻilani.” These West Maui bays were associated with Piʻilani, the ruling chief remembered for helping unify Maui and for strengthening islandwide routes of movement and connection. The modern Honoapiʻilani Highway preserves this older name and follows the coast near many of these same bays.

For Native Hawaiians, Honolua served as a safe harbor along a rugged shoreline. In later periods, ranching and plantation interests used the bay to receive supplies and ship agricultural products. Over time, the bay continued to serve as a protected coastal landing place, even as the surrounding lands shifted through ranching, pineapple cultivation, conservation, and resort development.

Honolua Bay also holds an important place in modern Hawaiian voyaging history. On May 1, 1976, Hōkūleʻa departed from Honolua on its first successful voyage to Tahiti, helping revive traditional Polynesian navigation and inspiring a broader cultural movement across Hawaiʻi and the Pacific.

Today, Honolua is known for its winter surf, summer snorkeling, coral reefs, and its place within the Honolua-Mokuleʻia Marine Life Conservation District. The bay’s modern reputation as a surf break and marine preserve continues to rest on older layers of meaning: twin waters, chiefly movement, safe harbor, and connection across the ocean.
